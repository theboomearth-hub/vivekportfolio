# Bengaluru rent and buy: desk research

> Research file for [02-desk-research.md](02-desk-research.md). Compiled 13 Nov 2026 with AI-assisted desk research; facts marked "snippet only" must be re-checked in a browser before publishing.

Prepared for a UX case study (agentic-first platform for renting or buying a home in Bengaluru). Research cut-off: **13 Nov 2026**.

## How to read this file

- Facts are numbered `Q.n`, where Q is the question number (1 to 15) from the brief. For example, **1.3** is the third fact for question 1.
- Each fact gives: the claim, the source (publisher, title, URL), the date, and a confidence note.
- **Access** says whether I opened the page myself (**fetched**) or only saw a search-engine extract because the site blocks automated readers (**snippet only**). Deccan Herald, The Hindu, Times of India, Economic Times, Mint, Moneycontrol, Housing.com and Business Standard all blocked fetching. Their figures appear only where nothing else was available, and they are marked "snippet only". Re-check them in a browser before publishing.
- **Confidence:**
  - **High**: an official source, or two independent reputable sources agree.
  - **Medium**: one reputable news or industry source.
  - **Low**: a platform or blog source, a snippet only, an anecdote, or a source that conflicts with others.
- Industry rent and price figures are **quoted (asking) values**, not transaction values, unless stated otherwise.

## A. Renting in Bengaluru

### Q1. Security deposit, and whether the law caps it

**1.1** Bengaluru landlords "often demand" deposits of **six to nine months' rent, sometimes even 12**. The article puts this at roughly ₹3 to 5 lakh per tenant. It also says the Model Tenancy Act (MTA) limits residential deposits to two months' rent but "has yet to be fully adopted in Karnataka."
- Source: Business Today, "'The biggest scam in Bengaluru...': Investment advisor calls out city's rental deposit trap", https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/real-estate/story/the-biggest-scam-in-bengaluru-investment-advisor-calls-out-citys-rental-deposit-trap-472022-2025-04-15
- Date: 15 Apr 2025. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: Medium. This is news reporting an investment advisor's characterisation, not survey data.

**1.2** Older NoBroker data: Bengaluru landlords demanded **eight months'** deposit on average, about ₹1,34,472 (Chennai: seven months).
- Source: Business Standard, "With rents pushing up, that apartment you had your eye on just got pricier", https://www.business-standard.com/article/pf/with-rents-pushing-up-that-apartment-you-had-your-eye-on-just-got-pricier-119010401193_1.html
- Date: Jan 2019 (from the URL id). Access: snippet only.
- Confidence: Low to Medium. **Outdated (2019)**. Use only as a historical baseline.

**1.3** A report attributed to NoBroker's "Rent Report 2026" says tenants in six major cities have about **₹1.26 lakh crore** locked in security deposits, with **₹31,628 crore in Bengaluru**.
- Source: RentOk (a rental-management company), "Security Deposits: The Hidden Cost of Renting", https://rentok.com/blogs/case-study/security-deposits-the-hidden-cost-of-renting
- Date: 2026 (exact date not seen). Access: snippet only.
- Confidence: Low. This is a secondary citation by a platform blog, and I did not check it against the NoBroker report itself.

**1.4** **Karnataka has not adopted the Model Tenancy Act, 2021.** MTA protections such as the two-month deposit cap and the Rent Authority are not in force there. The Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 "does not cap residential deposits". The "10 months" figure is local practice, not law.
- Source: Commoner Law (legal explainer), "Karnataka Tenant Rights Under the Model Tenancy Act Laws (2026)", https://commoner-law.com/india/housing-rights/tenant-rights/karnataka
- Date: 2026; accessed 13 Nov 2026. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: Medium. It is consistent with 1.5 and 1.6.

**1.5** The only recent change to Karnataka's rent law is the **Karnataka Rent (Amendment) Act, 2025**.
- Passage: the Assembly passed it on 16 Dec 2025 and the Council on 17 Dec 2025. The Governor assented on **7 Jan 2026**, and it was notified in the Karnataka Gazette on **8 Jan 2026**.
- Content: it **decriminalises** offences under the 1999 Act, replacing jail terms with civil penalties. The Rent Controller becomes the adjudicating officer for penalties, and penalties rise automatically by 10% every three years.
- What it leaves alone: a law-firm analysis says it leaves substantive matters, including "security deposit limits", governed by the principal Act. **It introduces no deposit cap.**
- Sources:
  - TeamLease RegTech, "Karnataka Rent (Amendment) Act, 2025", https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/51651/karnataka-rent-amendment-act-2025 (fetched)
  - Khaitan & Co via Legal500, "Changes introduced by the Karnataka Rent (Amendment) Act, 2025", https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/real-estate-and-construction/changes-introduced-by-the-karnataka-rent-amendment-act-2025 (20 Apr 2026, fetched)
  - The Week / PTI, "Karnataka Assembly clears 12 bills including Karnataka Rent Amendment Bill", https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/12/16/mds22-ka-assembly-bills-passed.html (16 Dec 2025, snippet only)
  - Council passage on 17 Dec 2025: Deccan Herald, "Council passes 11 bills", https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/council-passes-11-bills-2-3834771 (snippet only)
- Confidence: High.

**1.6** What the Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 actually says:
- **Section 11 ("Unlawful charges not to be claimed")** bars payments for giving up or transferring a tenancy. It does not set a general deposit limit. One numeric limit exists: a deposit that finances construction may not exceed **five years' agreed rent** (s.11(2)(b)).
- **Section 2(3)(e)** excludes premises whose rent exceeds **₹3,500/month in "Part A" areas**, which are city corporations under the Karnataka Municipal Corporations Act 1976, plus 3 km around them.
- **Section 2(3)(f)** excludes premises for **15 years after construction**.
- Source: CaseMine, "Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 (Karnataka Act 34 of 2001)" (bare-act text), https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5a9cccf34a932653478126e9
- Date: accessed 13 Nov 2026. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: Medium. This is a legal database copy, and the official India Code PDF blocked fetching. See Gaps G3 for what this implies.

**1.7 CONFLICT.** Business Today says that under the MTA a residential deposit "cannot exceed two months' rent" and that "Maharashtra and Karnataka have now aligned their rules with the MTA." This contradicts 1.4 to 1.6. Several SEO blogs also claim a two-month cap "from January 2026", for example realtyhunting.com and courtkutchehry.com.
- Source: Business Today, "Model Tenancy Act: How new security deposits, rent agreement, entry-exit rules will benefit tenants, landlords", https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/real-estate/story/model-tenancy-act-how-new-security-deposits-rent-agreement-entry-exit-rules-will-benefit-tenants-landlords-504839-2025-12-03
- Date: 3 Dec 2025. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: Low for the Karnataka claim. The enacted 2025 amendment (1.5) contains no deposit provision. **Do not publish "Karnataka caps deposits at two months."**

**1.8** A Deccan Herald headline reported that "Karnataka house owners may soon be barred from demanding more than two months' rent as advance". The government had announced plans to adopt the MTA. As of Nov 2026 this has not happened (see 1.4 and 1.5).
- Source: Deccan Herald, https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/karnataka-house-owners-may-soon-be-barred-from-demanding-more-than-two-months-rent-as-advance-1006391.html
- Date: not visible (coverage of the 2021 MTA announcement). Access: snippet only.
- Confidence: Low for the date. The headline itself is verifiable.

### Q2. Agreement length, stamp duty and registration

**2.1** Under the Registration Act, 1908, s.17(1)(d), registration is compulsory for "leases of immovable property from year to year, or for any term exceeding one year, or reserving a yearly rent". Agreements of 11 months fall outside this.
- Source: Indian Kanoon, "Section 17 in The Registration Act, 1908", https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1489134/
- Date: accessed 13 Nov 2026. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: High. This is the statute text, though Indian Kanoon is not the official gazette.

**2.2** Explainer: the **11-month** term exists because "agreements exceeding one year typically require registration", which many landlords prefer to avoid. Most Bengaluru rentals are leave-and-licence agreements. The article's Bellandur example: **₹35,000 rent with a ₹2.1 lakh deposit**, which is six months.
- Source: Swarajya, "Why every rental agreement in India is eleven months long", https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/legal/why-every-rental-agreement-in-india-is-eleven-months-long
- Date: 1 Apr 2026 (updated 8 Jun 2026). Access: fetched.
- Confidence: Medium.

**2.3** Stamp duty on a Karnataka residential rent agreement of one year or less falls under Article 30 of the Karnataka Stamp Act, 1957. Secondary sources commonly cite **0.5% of rent plus advance, capped at ₹500**. Other sources say a **₹200 e-stamp** is what is commonly used for 11-month agreements.
- Sources:
  - Housing.com, "Rental agreement stamp paper value and stamp duty in various states", https://housing.com/news/stamp-duty-on-rental-agreement/ (snippet only)
  - Vault PropTech blog, https://www.vaultproptech.com/blogs/stamp-paper-for-rental-agreement-karnataka (snippet only)
- Date: accessed 13 Nov 2026.
- Confidence: **Low.** I could not read the official Article 30 schedule. The 2023 amendment (2.5) may have changed it. Verify on the Kaveri / IGR Karnataka fee table.

**2.4** The Karnataka High Court (Justice S. Vishwajith Shetty) held that a **refundable security deposit is not "money advanced in addition to rent"** under Article 30(c). Stamp duty is therefore charged on the average annual rent, not on the deposit.
- Source: Rawlaw, "Karnataka High Court: Refundable Security Deposit Under Lease Not Liable for Stamp Duty", https://rawlaw.in/karnataka-high-court-refundable-security-deposit-under-lease-not-liable-for-stamp-duty-refundable-security-deposit-does-not-constitute-money-advanced-in-addition-to-rent/
- Date: 25 Oct 2025 (judgment date not stated). Access: fetched.
- Confidence: Medium. This is legal news, and I have not read the judgment.

**2.5** The **Karnataka Stamp (Amendment) Act, 2023** was notified on **3 Feb 2024**. It revised duty on many instruments (affidavits, conveyance, powers of attorney and others) and added a bank-guarantee article. The summary I read does not describe any change to leases.
- Source: Argus Partners via Legal500, "Overview of the Karnataka Stamp (Amendment) Act, 2023", https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/government-public-sector/overview-of-the-karnataka-stamp-amendment-act-2023
- Date: 1 Mar 2024 (republished 15 Apr 2024). Access: fetched.
- Confidence: Medium. Whether it changed rent-agreement duty is unknown.

### Q3. Brokerage

**3.1** Portal guidance says brokers charge **at least one month's rent, and up to three months in cities such as Mumbai and Bangalore**. Fees are higher in prime localities. Some brokers take half from the tenant and half from the landlord. Fees can rise to 45 days or two months for leases longer than a year. A tenant is "not legally bound" to pay brokerage on renewal.
- Source: Housing.com (platform blog), "What are the brokerage charges for renting a flat in India?", https://housing.com/news/what-are-the-brokerage-charges-for-renting-a-flat-in-india
- Date: accessed 13 Nov 2026. Access: snippet only.
- Confidence: Low. Platform blog, acceptable only as a "how it works" description.

**3.2** User forum answer: "there are no norms or laws in India for broker fees". Typical asks range from **half a month to two months** depending on urgency and negotiation. On who pays, forum answers conflict: some say the tenant pays if they used the broker, others say brokers charge both sides.
- Source: NoBroker forum, "What is the going broker fees, is it one month?", https://www.nobroker.in/forum/what-is-the-going-broker-fees-is-it-one-month
- Date: accessed 13 Nov 2026. Access: snippet only.
- Confidence: Low (forum).

**3.3** Karnataka RERA's portal has an **agent status lookup** (`/viewAllAgents`). RERA agent registration covers agents who facilitate the **sale** of units in RERA projects. It does not regulate rental brokerage.
- Source: Karnataka RERA, https://rera.karnataka.gov.in/
- Date: accessed 13 Nov 2026. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: High that the lookup exists. The note on scope is my reading of the RERA framework.

## B. Buying in Bengaluru

### Q5. Stamp duty, registration fee and guidance value

**5.1** Karnataka **doubled the property registration fee from 1% to 2%** of value. The notification was dated 29 Aug 2025 under the Registration Act, 1908, and took effect **31 Aug 2025**. Stamp duty for transactions above ₹45 lakh **stayed at 5%**.
- Sources:
  - King Stubb & Kasiva (law firm), "Karnataka doubles property registration fee in 2025", https://ksandk.com/md/newsletter/karnataka-doubles-property-registration-fee-in-2025/ (15 Sep 2025, updated 27 May 2026, fetched)
  - Corroborated by Deccan Herald, "Property deals to cost more: registration fee hiked to 2%", https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/property-deals-to-cost-more-registration-fee-hiked-to-2-2-3704857 (Aug 2025, snippet only)
- Confidence: High.

**5.2** Typical all-in statutory cost for a Bengaluru purchase above ₹45 lakh is about **7.6%**: 5% stamp duty, 2% registration and about 0.6% cess and charges. Example: a ₹1 crore flat costs about ₹7.6 lakh in statutory charges.
- Source: Angel One (broker news desk), "2% Hike in Karnataka's Property Registration Fee: Impact on Bengaluru Homebuyers", https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/2-hike-in-karnataka-s-property-registration-fee-impact-on-bengaluru-homebuyers
- Date: 4 Sep 2025. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: Medium. Other outlets say about 7.5%, depending on how cess and surcharge are rounded.

**5.3** Stamp duty and registration fee are charged on **the higher of the guidance value or the declared sale price**. Under-reporting attracts penalties.
- Source: King Stubb & Kasiva, as in 5.1.
- Confidence: High. This is standard Karnataka practice and consistent across sources.

**5.4** **Concessional stamp duty for new apartments (first registration only):**
- 2% for new flats under ₹20 lakh (2020-21 budget proposal).
- 3% (from 5%) for apartments up to ₹45 lakh (cabinet decision of July 2021). The minister said it applies only "for the first registration of these apartments".
- Sources:
  - The News Minute, "Karnataka govt cuts stamp duty for apartments below Rs 45 lakh", https://www.thenewsminute.com/karnataka/karnataka-govt-cuts-stamp-duty-apartments-below-rs-45-lakh-experts-welcome-move-152854 (24 Jul 2021, fetched)
  - DT Next, "K'taka Budget cuts stamp duty on flats to 3pc", https://www.dtnext.in/news/national/ktaka-budget-cuts-stamp-duty-on-flats-to-3pc (8 Mar 2021, fetched)
  - The News Minute, "Stamp duty on new flats costing less than Rs 20 lakh to be reduced to 2%", https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/stamp-duty-new-flats-costing-less-rs-20-lakh-be-reduced-2-yediyurappa-119560 (2020, snippet only)
- Confidence: Medium for the history. **Low for whether these slabs still apply in 2026.** I could not confirm current status, and King Stubb & Kasiva says only that "lower-value properties get concessional rates".

**5.5** **Guidance values** (government minimum values) were last confirmed revised statewide **effective 1 Oct 2023**. The Revenue Minister said the average increase would be **25 to 30%**.
- Source: Udayavani, "Implementation of revised property guidance value from October 1: Karnataka Revenue Minister", https://www.udayavani.com/english-news/implementation-of-revised-property-guidance-value-from-october-1-karnataka-revenue-minister
- Date: Sep 2023. Access: snippet only.
- Confidence: Medium.

**5.6** A further guidance-value revision was reported as **proposed for April 2026** (Deccan Herald headline: "Property deals across Karnataka may get costlier from April"). Blog sources from May 2026 say it had **not been gazette-notified**. Its status as of Nov 2026 was **not confirmed**.
- Source: Deccan Herald, https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/property-deals-across-karnataka-may-get-costlier-from-april-3913337
- Date: not visible (c. Feb 2026, judging from related coverage). Access: snippet only.

### Q6. GST on under-construction homes

**6.1** From **1 Apr 2019**, under-construction residential apartments carry GST of **1% (affordable) or 5% (other)**, both **without input tax credit**. The government's FAQ refers to the "option of 1% or 5% (without ITC)" against the older 8% and 12% with ITC.
- Source: Ministry of Finance, Tax Research Unit (via GST Council), "FAQs (Part II) on real estate", F. No. 354/32/2019-TRU, https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/faq_ii_real-estate-sector.pdf
- Date: 14 May 2019. Access: fetched (text extracted locally).
- Confidence: High (official).

**6.2** An "affordable residential apartment" has a **carpet area under 60 sq m in specified metropolitan cities** (90 sq m elsewhere) and a gross amount of **no more than ₹45 lakh**. Bengaluru is one of the specified metros under Notification 3/2019-CT(R), alongside Chennai, Delhi NCR, Hyderabad, Kolkata and Mumbai MMR.
- Sources:
  - The 60/90 sq m and ₹45 lakh test: same FAQ as 6.1 (Q5 of the FAQ). Confidence: High.
  - The list of metros: TaxGuru, https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-construction-affordable-residential-apartments-mumbai-metropolitan-region.html (snippet only). Confidence: Medium.

**6.3** **No GST on ready-to-move homes:** sale of a building is outside GST when **the entire consideration is received after the completion certificate is issued or after first occupation, whichever is earlier**. This comes from CGST Act Schedule II para 5(b) read with Schedule III.
- Source: TaxTMI commentary quoting the statute, https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_blog_details?id=652604
- Date: accessed 13 Nov 2026. Access: snippet only.
- Confidence: Medium. The rule is well established, but I did not open the official CGST Act text.

**6.4** The **GST 2.0** rate rationalisation (effective 22 Sep 2025) left residential property rates unchanged and cut GST on **cement from 28% to 18%**.
- Source: Business Today, "GST 2.0 on housing: CA warns simplification doesn't mean affordability for all homebuyers", https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/real-estate/story/gst-20-on-housing-ca-warns-simplification-doesnt-mean-affordability-for-all-homebuyers-497735-2025-10-10
- Date: 10 Oct 2025. Access: snippet only.
- Confidence: Medium.

### Q7. Karnataka RERA

**7.1** Under the RERA Act, 2016, s.3(1), no promoter may "advertise, market, book, sell or offer for sale" units in a project without registering it with the state RERA. Exemptions under s.3(2):
- land of **500 sq m or less, or 8 apartments or fewer** (counting all phases)
- projects that had a completion certificate before the Act commenced
- renovation or repair with no new sale

Each phase counts as a separate project.
- Source: AdvocateKhoj (bare act), "Section 3, Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016", https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/realestate2016/3.php
- Date: accessed 13 Nov 2026. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: High. Note: state authorities have read the "or" in the small-project exemption differently. See Mondaq / Argus Partners, "RERA regime: the exemption conundrum", https://www.mondaq.com/india/real-estate/1326236/rera-regime-the-exemption-conundrum (snippet only).

**7.2** **How a buyer checks a project:** on Karnataka RERA's official portal **https://rera.karnataka.gov.in/**, use "Know Project Status" (https://rera.karnataka.gov.in/viewAllProjects). The portal also has:
- a "Default Project List" (`/viewDefaultProjects`)
- agent lookup (`/viewAllAgents`)
- complaint registration (`/complaintHome`)
- complaint status (`/viewAllComplaints`)

The homepage counter showed **10,652** projects that had applied for registration.
- Source: Karnataka RERA (official).
- Date: accessed 13 Nov 2026. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: High. The counter is a point-in-time figure.

### Q8. Khata: A-khata, B-khata and e-khata

**8.1** **A-khata** is "an official property record identifying ownership, size, and tax liabilities" for properties that are "fully approved and legally compliant". **B-khata** properties "often have building deviations or pending approvals, limiting their access to bank loans or resale opportunities".
- Source: ETV Bharat, "Karnataka's B-Khata To A-Khata Scheme Sees Poor Start: Only 1,169 Applications In First Week", https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/karnataka-b-khata-to-a-khata-scheme-sees-poor-start-enn25111204591
- Date: 12 Nov 2025. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: Medium.

**8.2** **e-khata became mandatory for property sale and mortgage transactions in October 2024.** From **1 Jul 2025** it (or an ePID) is also required for online building-plan applications. About **5 lakh e-khatas** had been issued by June 2025.
- Source: The News Minute, "E-Khata mandatory for building plan approvals in Bengaluru from July 1", https://www.thenewsminute.com/karnataka/e-khata-mandatory-for-building-plan-approvals-in-bengaluru-from-july-1
- Date: Jun 2025. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: Medium to High. Other sources give 30 Sep 2024, or 13 Sep 2024 for BDA properties, as the start date.

**8.3** e-khata is "now mandatory for all property transactions, including sale and resale, in sub-registrar offices". **A and B khata properties both get e-khatas** (A-e-Khata and B-e-Khata), but e-khata "does not regularise B khata properties".
- Portal: **https://bbmpeaasthi.karnataka.gov.in** (e-Aasthi).
- Inputs needed: Aadhaar, sale-deed registration number (fetched from Kaveri), property tax ID, BESCOM number, and a property photo.
- Source: Citizen Matters, "Frequently Asked Questions on BBMP e-khata", https://citizenmatters.in/frequently-asked-questions-on-bbmp-e-khata/
- Date: 28 Dec 2024. Access: fetched.
- Confidence: Medium to High. Note that the Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) has since replaced BBMP, so portal branding may change.

**8.4** **B-khata to A-khata conversion:**
- The Greater Bengaluru Authority announced a **one-time scheme** on 15 Oct 2025, run as a **100-day campaign from 1 Nov 2025**.
- Eligibility: sites up to 2,000 sq m apply online; larger sites apply through an architect on the BPAS portal. **B-khata flats and multi-storey units are not eligible.**
- Fee: **5% of the site's guidance value**.
- Scale: Bengaluru has about **25 lakh properties, about 7.5 lakh of them B-khata**.
- Uptake: in the first week only **1,169 applications** arrived. An e-khata is a prerequisite, and only about **2.6 lakh** B-khata properties had one.

## Gaps and conflicts

**Conflicts to resolve before publishing**

- **G1. Deposit cap.** Business Today (1.7) and several blogs say Karnataka aligned with the MTA's two-month cap. Legal analyses (1.4, 1.5) and the Act's text (1.6) say there is no cap, and the 2025 amendment only decriminalised offences. Treat "no statutory cap; 6 to 10 months is market practice" as the defensible position.
- **G2. Rent growth.** Anarock shows 0 to 1% QoQ quoted rents in H1 2026 (11.1, 11.2). Magicbricks shows +8.6% QoQ in Q1 2026 (11.6). NoBroker shows +8% for H1 2025 (11.5). The methods differ (asking rents in a few micro-markets, versus portal listings, versus platform transactions). Cite each with its method rather than one "city rent growth" number.
- **G3. Scope of the Rent Act (my inference, not a sourced claim).** Section 2(3)(e)–(f) excludes premises renting above ₹3,500/month in Part A areas and buildings under 15 years old. Most modern Bengaluru flats are therefore probably outside the Act and governed only by contract. Whether Bengaluru (now under the Greater Bengaluru Governance framework) still counts as "Part A" was not verified. Get a lawyer's confirmation before stating it.
- **G4. Price growth.** For 2026 the sources give: Anarock +8% YoY (Q3), Knight Frank +9% (H1), PropTiger +24% (Q1). These are different baselines and samples.
- **G5. Metro deadlines.** The Pink underground section has two targets (May vs Sep 2027), and the Blue Line has several (Jun 2027, Dec 2027, Dec 2028).
- **G6. e-khata start date.** Sources give 13 Sep 2024 (BDA), 30 Sep 2024, and October 2024.
- **G7. TomTom figures.** The annual average (36:09 per 10 km) differs from the peak figures quoted in local coverage (38 min, and 41 to 45 min). Bengaluru Traffic Police disputes the numbers.
- **G8. Total statutory purchase cost.** Sources give 7.5% to 7.6% depending on how cess and surcharge are counted.
